Field notes

Setting a field guide for the screen

A print field guide survives rain, mud and a reader in a hurry. Its web edition has to survive phones, zoom and three languages.

The print edition of the guide (1990–2004) was set by hand. Every entry had to fit a single spread, so the type was small, the leading tight and the measure narrow. Readers carried it into the field and read it standing up, often in poor light. None of that was an accident: the “shape” of the page was a decision made for a particular reader.

The web edition inherits the text but not the decisions. A browser will happily set the same paragraph across 1,400 pixels, justify it into rivers of white and shrink the headings when the reader zooms. The defaults were not chosen for anyone; the reader notices only that the page is tiring.

What the readers asked for

We asked 120 readers of the print guide what they missed. Most named the same three things: lines short enough to follow, numbers that line up and headings that stay where they expect them. Several mentioned that the WCAG guidance on zoom was the first thing that broke on their phones.

“I don’t want it to look like the book. I want it to read like the book.”

That sentence became the brief. Reading like the book meant a measure of about 66 characters, a body line that every gap is measured in and figures that sit with the text in prose and line up in tables.
